## One-breath model

FVC funds UK SMEs without charging interest. Each business enters a Musharakah or Mudarabah agreement and shares its profit with the treasury. Part of those returns can be paid to FVC holders who stake. SMEs receive funding and repay in pounds through our payments partner, with the Loan Registry recording each move.

**Own money (say this when someone says fund / CIS):**  
We are not raising a fund that lends other people's money. It is our own money.

---

## Structure and Islamic financing

- Contracts will use Musharakah and Mudarabah structures in line with traditional Islamic financing structures.
- Interest-free. Profit-sharing with the treasury, not interest on a loan.
- Company capital on the company balance sheet. FVC Asset Group Ltd owns the SME agreements and takes the credit risk.

**Musharakah** (partnership / joint venture): FVC and the SME put capital into a deal together and share profit and loss by agreement. Both sides have skin in the game.

**Mudarabah** (capital provider + manager): FVC puts up the capital; the SME runs the business. Profit is shared by an agreed split. Where there is a loss with no fault, FVC typically bears the capital loss; the SME’s loss is the effort/time.

**Call line:** We don’t charge interest. We use Musharakah or Mudarabah: the business shares profit with the treasury instead of paying interest.

## Staking (accurate tense)

- Staking is built to distribute USDC from lending **income**, not principal.
- Principal stays available to lend again.
- Staking is not yet deployed on mainnet (`SECURITY.md`). Say “built to” / “designed to”, not “already pays”, until it is live.
- A staker can earn nothing until signers send income to the staking contract.
