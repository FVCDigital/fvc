Ancova: facts for Thursday 23 October 2026

For Abdul. First meeting. Internal notes, not a script to read out.


1. THE SHORT VERSION

What this section is for: everything Abdul needs if he reads only one paragraph.

Ancova is a Dubai wealth firm with a Cayman fund. They are not a venture fund that leads startup rounds. All-Stars, the product in the factsheet, buys and sells large US shares and stock options: about 30 to 40 names, roughly two-thirds owned and one-third sold short, with options as a hedge.

The split they show clients is a comparison, not the fund's holdings: property at about 4.7% a year, shares at about 9%, bonds at about 3.2%, and All-Stars at 14.5%. That 9% is a long-run average. Over 2017 to 2025 the S&P 500 with dividends was about 15% a year.

On their own table, $1 million in All-Stars after fees became $3.39 million. The same $1 million in the S&P 500 with dividends became about $3.56 million. Their gross figures beat the price-only S&P in 7 of 9 years. 2022 is the year they will talk about: the fund was up 11.9% while the index fell 19.4%.

The door that fits FVC is the other one. They also host outside managers, and they already name venture and digital assets among those strategies.

They do not lead startup funding rounds like a typical venture capital firm.


5. HOW THE ALL-STARS FUND WORKS

What this section is for: what the fund actually holds, in plain words.

- 30 to 40 large US companies, chosen from the S&P 500.
- Roughly two-thirds of those positions are owned outright. Roughly one-third are sold short, which means they make money if that share falls.
- Stock options sit on top as insurance against falls.
- Trading decisions are made by computer models (AI and statistical models), not by a manager picking stocks by hand.
- It still moves with the US stock market. It is not market-neutral, so it tends to lag in strong bull years.

8. FEES AND INVESTMENT TERMS

What this section is for: what it costs and how money goes in and out, in case they pitch the fund to us.

- Minimum investment: $1 million.
- Entry fee: 2.5%. No annual management fee.
- Performance fee: nothing on the first 6% of each year's gain, 30% of the gain between 6% and 10%, and 50% of anything above 10%.
- Withdrawals: quarterly, with 30 days' notice.

11. WHAT WE CAN OFFER THEM

What this section is for: what Abdul brings to the conversation. Check every figure with Abdul before he says it out loud.

- A different kind of investment for their clients: interest-free funding of small and medium-sized businesses through revenue-share agreements, rather than another US share fund.
- Sharia-friendly by design. Capital goes out interest-free, and the business pays back a share of its revenue. That is the structure Muslim family offices in Dubai already use, and FVC is built that way from the start. For a Gulf wealth firm, this is a product their clients can actually hold.
- Live on Ethereum since March 2026: the token, the sale, and vesting, with real investor allocations in them.
- In build now, for the payments partner. Traditional businesses repay in pounds or Swiss francs. His firm converts that into a pound- or franc-backed stablecoin and sends it to FVC's treasury. Two contracts make that sign-up real:
  - Loan registry: the public record of each funding agreement and each repayment, so every settlement can be matched to the right business. The design is written. This is the contract his compliance team needs before repayments can run. Do not say it is live.
  - Staking: already written and tested. It pays those repayments out to token holders. It goes on Ethereum with the first repayment, not before.
- An independent audit by Hashlock is underway. No final report yet, so do not say "audited".
- Real-world asset exposure, which their clients cannot get through All-Stars.
